# econ5200-lab02-deflation

# Index Integrity — Deflation, Substitution Bias & Goodhart

## Objective

Audit and repair a real-dollar deflation pipeline, quantify the upper-level substitution wedge between the CPI-U and the C-CPI-U in percentage points per year, and demonstrate Goodhart's Law empirically as a sign reversal in the relationship between a targeted engagement KPI and the underlying behavior it was meant to proxy.

## Methodology

- **Diagnostic-first repair of `deflate_series()`.** Isolated and corrected four defects in the deflation routine, the most consequential being a base-period mislabelling in which output denominated in 1982–84 dollars was reported as 2020 dollars — an error that leaves the *shape* of the deflated series intact while rescaling its level, and therefore survives most eyeball checks.
- **Rate-based measurement of substitution bias.** Compared the CPI-U against the C-CPI-U as annualized inflation rates rather than index levels, computing the wedge in percentage points per year. Deliberately contrasted this with the naive index-point-per-year spread and documented why the latter is not a rate: an index point is a level-dependent quantity, and dividing a cumulative spread by elapsed years imposes a linear average on a divergence generated by compounding.
- **Index-comparability controls.** Benchmarked the C-CPI-U against the *unadjusted* CPI-U (CPIAUCNS), since the C-CPI-U is published without seasonal adjustment, and rebased both series to a common start date before any level comparison — the two indices carry different reference bases (1982–84=100 vs. December 1999=100) and are not comparable in raw form.
- **Goodhart detection via rolling correlation.** Tracked the rolling correlation between DAU/MAU and time per session, treating a sign reversal as the empirical signature of a proxy decoupling from its target once the proxy became the objective. Window length was treated as an explicit detection-speed/noise tradeoff rather than a default.
- **Productionization.** Extracted the corrected logic into `deflation_utils.py`, exposing a tested `deflate_series()` with unit coverage on the base-period and rebasing paths that produced the original failures.
- **Interactive index-integrity monitor.** Built a single-cell `ipywidgets` + `plotly` monitor with a start-date picker (recomputing the gap in both index points per year and pp per year), a CPIAUCNS/CPIAUCSL toggle, a rolling-window slider for the KPI correlation, and an alert identifying the first window in which the correlation turns negative.

## Key Findings

- **Silent base-period corruption is the dominant failure mode.** Of the four defects, the 1982–84-dollars-as-2020-dollars mislabelling was the only one that produced no visible anomaly in the deflated series. Trend, turning points, and growth rates were all preserved; only the level was wrong. Any validation strategy that checks series shape rather than base-period identity would have passed it.
- **Upper-level substitution bias is economically material.** Over the sample window, the CPI-U rose at [FILL: CPI-U annualized rate]% per year against [FILL: C-CPI-U annualized rate]% per year for the C-CPI-U, a wedge of **[FILL: gap in pp] pp per year**. This corresponds to the upper-level substitution component identified by the Boskin Commission: the CPI-U's Laspeyres-type aggregation across item strata holds expenditure weights fixed, while the C-CPI-U's superlative (Törnqvist) formula allows weights to respond to relative price movements.
- **The index-point spread overstates the wedge and is not comparable across windows.** The same divergence reads as [FILL: index-point gap] index points per year, a figure that drifts upward as the window lengthens even when the underlying annual wedge is constant. Reported as a rate, the measure is stable; reported as points, it is an artifact of window choice and base level.
- **The engagement KPI decoupled from the behavior it proxied.** The rolling correlation between DAU/MAU and time per session moved from **[FILL: initial correlation]** to **[FILL: post-flip correlation]**, reversing sign. Sessions became more frequent and shorter: the metric improved while the construct it stood in for deteriorated — a textbook Goodhart outcome, and an argument for monitoring proxy–target correlation as a first-class alert rather than monitoring the proxy alone.
- **Operational implication.** When the correlation alert fires, the appropriate response is to freeze the proxy as a decision input and re-derive the target measure directly, not to retune the threshold. A proxy that has decoupled does not recouple by recalibration.
